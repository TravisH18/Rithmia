Lure of Jaws

Curdelgyth, The Dream Carnivore​

Curdelgyth is an old green dragon, aside from the usual characteristics, has very crooked teeth and stays low to the ground like a crouching tiger stalking its prey. It hunted the forests as its true apex predator but got too greedy and devoured one of the last herds of a now extinct fry creature and was cursed by the witch queen iggwilv in retaliation Tasha cursed curdelgyth and her clutch of eggs making them all spawn as green jabberwocks.

Iggwilv’s past in the abyss allowed her access to a potent curse. She drew out Curdelgyth’s evil heart and trapped it in a glass box. 1/day curdelgyth can unleash the negative energy in her heart. Emanating an aura of gluttony (condition).

If curdelgyth is separated from the necklace (it is out of range) she has to use her full movement to approach it and get it back and has disadvantage on wisdom checks and saving throws

Mannerisms: punctuates speech with growls, roars, and small puffs of green smoke. Tail constantly twitches like its ready to pounce.

Goal: Kill and consume in fey areas to hope to confront the archfey that cursed her

Discover powerful alchemists and brilliant minds to help her craft the a legendary potion, Grey Matter, the Neuronifier that is said to be able to cure any mental affliciton. In addition it sharpens the wit of any creature but seems to be more effective on older creatures as it acts like a clean up and organizational service that sharpens the mind by cleaning and organizing.

Zundrak pledged to help with both goals.

Death throes: Roll on wild magic surge table

Energy of the lair enters them: Give them a dark gift from van richten books

Lure of Jaws​

Curdelgyth's Lair is the Lure of Jaws Built in a steep jagged mountain in the eastern tip of the earthfast mountains the green dragon Curdelgyth kidnapped and forced a group of 2 dozen dwarves to make her a lair filled with traps. She brought her entire hoard here to entice adventures to visit to satiate her never ending hunger. After she was cursed by the witch queen iggwilv (Tasha). The main hoard area is under the Private Sanctum spell and the entire mountain is under the forbiddance spell. If forbiddance is dispeled minions are sent hunting through the lair.

It is widely known that curdelgyth built this lair centuries ago however the last sighting of the dragon was before the slumber of wyrms and they say the dragon had gone mad. The deadly traps have thwarted adventurers for years as none who have entered have been seen again.

Curdelgyth uses this lair as a way of both trapping food and finding new powerful subjects.

The labyrinth built above her hoard is meant to sort parties by ability and intellect and she knows their ability by what tunnel they enter through when making the final descent to her hoard. Weaker foes are treated like prey and hunted without warning. Capable foes will be met with the malicious green of a fey cursed ancient dragon who offers a small bit of gold for joining her in her quest for revenge against the wretched withces of the fey wild that cursed her and deformed her childs body and mind. Her armies conquer fey forests and capturing all creatures with even a shred of fey ancestory (genasi bois have the fey stench but they don't as good a flavor as elves) in the hopes of finding a rift to the feywild or someone with a connection to Baba Yaga's child.

Speculators believe the dragon is the only one who knows of a secret escape tunnel that allows them to move in and out of the lair unnoticed but nothing has ever been found. (Tunnel starts in deep in cave and passes through the feywild before putting exiting into the north eastern swampy marshlands).

Regional Effects​

  • Thickets form labyrinthine passages within 1 mile of the dragon’s lair. The thickets act as 10-foot-high, 10-foot-thick walls that block line of sight. Creatures can move through the thickets, with every 1 foot a creature moves costing it 4 feet of movement. A creature in the thickets must make a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw once each round it’s in contact with the thickets or take 3 (1d6)piercing damage from thorns. Each 10-foot-cube of thickets has AC 5, 30 hit points, resistance to bludgeoning and piercing damage, vulnerability to fire damage, and immunity to psychic and thunder damage.
    • Acts like a maze spell
  • The same thickets that form the labyrinth entice all who come in range to delve deeper into the thicket. Constantly whispering and drawing the characters in. All those within 60ft of the edge of the brush naturally begin to walk towards the brush with no thought behind it. A character who chooses to resist must make a DC 15 Wisdom saving throw or walk towards the center of the brush as if under the compulsion spell (won't endanger itself feels as it is under its own will.
  • Within 1 mile of its lair, the dragon leaves no physical evidence of its passage unless it wishes to. Tracking it there is impossible except by magical means. In addition, it ignores movement impediments and damage from plants in this area that are neither magical nor creatures, including the thickets described above. The plants remove themselves from the dragon’s path.

A pillar of light stretches the location. The trap is activated if the beam is broken by a creature or object and makes an iron gate drop from the ceiling some distance behind, blocking the exit.

Effect: DC 15 to find, DC 10 to disable

Targets all creatures within a 10 ft. square area, An iron gate drops from the ceiling some distance behind the trigger, blocking the exit. Any target underneath the gate makes a DC 11 DEX save or takes 22 (4d10) bludgeoning damage. Have each character roll a d100 0-49 go to E1, 50-99 go to T2

T2: Poisoned Tempest

Complex trap (level 11–16, deadly threat) This fiendish trap was built to eliminate intruders who infiltrate a yuan-ti temple. The trap is a room, 60-foot square with 5-foot-wide stone doors in the middle of each wall. In each corner of the room stands a 10-foot-tall statue of a great serpent, coiled and ready to strike. The eyes in each statue are rubies worth 200 gp a piece.

Trigger. This trap activates when a ruby is pried from one of the statues. Each statue’s

mouth slides open, revealing a 1-foot-wide pipe running down its throat.

Initiative. The trap acts on initiative 20 and 10 (but see the dynamic element below).

Active Elements. The Poisoned Tempest fills the room with poison and other deadly effects.

Locked Doors (Initiative 20). The four doors to this room slam shut and are locked in place by magic. This effect activates once, the first time the trap activates.

Poison Gas (Initiative 20). Poison gas floods the room. Each creature inside must make a

DC 20 Constitution saving throw, taking 33 (6d10) poison damage on a failed save, or half

as much damage on a successful one.

Tempest (Initiative 10). A mixture of air and gas boils up from the trap. Roll a d6 and

consult the Tempest Effects table.

Tempest Effects

d6 Effect

1 Hallucinatory gas scrambles the mind and senses. All Intelligence and Wisdom checks

made in the room suffer disadvantage until the Tempest element activates again.

2 Explosive gas fills the area. If anyone holds an open flame, it explodes and deals 22 (4d10)

flames are then extinguished.

3 Weakening gas fills the room. All Strength and Dexterity checks made in the room suffer

4 Buffeting winds force everyone in the room to succeed on a DC 20 Strength saving throw or

Poison Gas element.

Dynamic Element—Increased Potency. The damage from the Poison Gas element increases

by 11 (2d10) after it activates, to a maximum of 55 (10d10).

Countermeasures. There are a few ways that the trap can be overcome. Open the Doors. Opening the doors is the quickest way to escape the trap, but they are

warded with magic. To open the doors, the characters must first make a DC 20 Wisdom

(Perception) check to find the locking mechanism. A DC 20 Intelligence (Arcana)

check disables the sphere of force that surrounds the lock (dispel magic is ineffective

against it). Finally, a DC 20 Dexterity check made with thieves’ tools picks the lock. Finally,

a DC 20 Strength (Athletics) check is needed to push the door open. Each check requires an

action.

Disable the Statues. A statue can be disabled by blocking the flow of gas from its mouth.

Destroying the statue is a bad idea, for it leaves the gas vents open. A DC 20 Strength check or

an attack that deals more than 20 damage to a statue cracks it and increases the Poison Gas

damage by 5 (1d10). A successful DC 20 Dexterity check with thieves’ tools, or a DC 15

Strength check made to block up the statue with a cloak or similar object, decreases the

poison damage by 5 (1d10). Once a character succeeds at the check, someone must remain

next to the statue to keep it blocked up. Once all four statues are blocked in this manner, the

trap deactivates.

Puzzles​

Fey Astronomy

Difficulty: hard

This puzzle features words inscribed in glowing letters upon the lower sections of the wall. Puzzle Features

If a character approaches the dials, arcane glyphs flash to life and glow scarlet around the edge of the stone plinth to reveal an inscription. The inscription reads as follows:

“The fey count numbers and words like stars:

Eight stands above, blazing eternal like a ruby,

While Three lights the way for you only.”

Examining the walls reveals the same phrases as the glowing letters have also been carved into the walls behind the glyphs and traced in crimson paint. The base of the round chamber also features six rotatable dials set into the stone, each colored to match the glimmering stars and featuring the numbers 0 through 30 written around their edges. A triangular marker at the outside and center point toward each other, indicating the number “0” on each of the dials currently. If the dials are turned, there is no perceivable change in the room unless the solution is found.

When reviewing the illusory stars along the wall, a simple count reveals the following stars: 3 diamonds, 8 emeralds, 12 amethysts, 16 sapphires, 24 rubies, 30 topazes. Attempting to touch the stars reveals that they are incorporeal, with the exception of the red stars: these are actually rubies embedded in the walls.

A detect magic spell will reveal that the arcane glyphs are illusions, as are all the gleaming stars except the rubies.

Solution

A character that turns the red dial to the number 24 but leaves the others untouched (following the instructions of the third and eighth word in the inscription: “Count Stars Above Ruby Lights Only”) is rewarded by the opening of a secret nigh undetectable door on the western side of the chamber

If any of the other dials are adjusted from the zero position, the door remains inactive until they are reset to their nominal position.

Hint Checks

If the players are having difficulty solving the puzzle, their characters can make checks to receive hints:

Wisdom (Insight) DC 15. The inscription seems to provide meaningless instruction, but it does appear to be worded specifically to feature three lines with eight words each.

Intelligence (Investigation) DC 10. The carved words in the walls appear to emphasize the third and eighth words in each line of the inscription.

Intelligence (History) DC 15. Many scholars agree that fey creatures place unusual importance on numerology, and there is often hidden significance in them.

Dragon Elements

Difficulty: medium

A 16-sided table rests in the center of a chamber, adorned with dragon skulls in each corner and a cluster of vials at the center.

The square chamber is small and lit by a circular skylight in the ceiling, granting ample light to illumine the decagonal stone table at its center. A faded and peeling fresco adorns the domed ceiling around the skylight, depicting dragons wielding devastating power over helpless creatures quavering before their might. A dragon’s head is carved at each corner of the stone table, painted a different color with jaws gaping open. The center of the table cluster five vials filled to the brim with arcane elemental energy, and around the collection of glass containers gilded letters read: “Feed each dragon their power.”

"Each skull may drink only twice a third and you're thrown to the vice" (Vine grabs them and throws them to crushing pit DC25 Dex save to avoid)

Puzzle Features

The 10 vials at the center of the table contain

bubbling acid

murky poison

crackling lightning

burning fire

shattered ice

Radiant light

Pink psychic mist

A dark black singularity

A dark cloud of thrumming thunder

Thick necrotic ichor A dark black singularity (This one requires a DC20 strength check to pour)

A dark cloud of thrumming thunder (When pouring make DC 20 Constituiton save or everyone is deafened for 1 minute and takes 3d8 thunder damage)

Thick necrotic ichor (If touches skin does 3d10 necrotic damage like inflict wounds) If the contents of a vial are poured out, they appear to magically reappear inside the container again. A detect magic spell reveals that the glass vials appear to be products of transmutation magic.

Each of the 15 dragon skulls is uniquely painted and carved to resemble common draconic ancestries: black, blue, brass, bronze, copper, gold, green, red, silver, white, amethyst, crystal, emerald, moonstone, sapphire, and topaz. The size of each skull is approximately the same as a wyrmling's but with empty eye sockets.

Solution

Solving the puzzle requires a character to pour the elemental contents of the vials into the dragon skull whose color corresponds to that element:

ice for silver and white

acid for black and copper

lightning for the blue and bronze dragons

poison for green

fire for the brass, gold, and red skulls.

Radiant for crystal, moonstone

Psychic for emerald

Gravity for Amethyst

Thunder for Sapphire

Necrotic for Topaz

Upon successfully providing a correct element to the corresponding dragon skull, the skull’s gaping maw slowly closes, its eyes blazing with the arcane element it ingested. Once all 16 skulls’ eyes are glowing, the tabletop rises upwards to reveal a hidden space beneath that can house an appropriate reward, such as a scroll, key, or map. As well as the opening of the correct manhole

Wisdom (Perception) DC 10. The fresco of dragons features 16 dragons altogether, and each is colored to correspond to one of the skulls set into the table.

Intelligence (Investigation) DC 20. Upon closer examination of the ancient fresco, a player can discover that the faded painting depicts the dragons destroying humanoids with their breath weapons (see the solution above for details regarding this). However some of them are ripped crumbled off the wall. This reveals 8 of the answers.

If they peel the fresco off the ceiling they find 10 different color gems corresponding to an element. They learn how many dragons go to each element (1 thunder 1 grav etc.)
