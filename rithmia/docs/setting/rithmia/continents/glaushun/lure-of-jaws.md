### Lure of Jaws
Curdelgyth's Lair is the Lure of Jaws
Built in a steep jagged mountain in the eastern tip of the earthfast mountains the green dragon Curdelgyth kidnapped and forced a group of 2 dozen dwarves to make her a lair filled with traps. She brought her entire hoard here to entice adventures to visit to satiate her never ending hunger. After she was cursed by the witch queen iggwilv (Tasha). The main hoard area is under the Private Sanctum spell and the entire mountain is under the forbiddance spell. If forbiddance is dispeled minions are sent hunting through the lair.  

It is widely known that curdelgyth built this lair centuries ago however the last sighting of the dragon was before the slumber of wyrms and they say the dragon had gone mad. The deadly traps have thwarted adventurers for years as none who have entered have been seen again. 

Curdelgyth uses this lair as a way of both trapping food and finding new powerful subjects. 

The labyrinth built above her hoard is meant to sort parties by ability and intellect and she knows their ability by what tunnel they enter through when making the final descent to her hoard. Weaker foes are treated like prey and hunted without warning. ### Puzzles
Fey Astronomy 

Difficulty: hard 

This puzzle features words inscribed in glowing letters upon the lower sections of the wall. 
Puzzle Features 

If a character approaches the dials, arcane glyphs flash to life and glow scarlet around the edge of the stone plinth to reveal an inscription. The inscription reads as follows: 

“The fey count numbers and words like stars: 

Eight stands above, blazing eternal like a ruby, 

While Three lights the way for you only.” 

Examining the walls reveals the same phrases as the glowing letters have also been carved into the walls behind the glyphs and traced in crimson paint. The base of the round chamber also features six rotatable dials set into the stone, each colored to match the glimmering stars and featuring the numbers 0 through 30 written around their edges. A triangular marker at the outside and center point toward each other, indicating the number “0” on each of the dials currently. If the dials are turned, there is no perceivable change in the room unless the solution is found. 

When reviewing the illusory stars along the wall, a simple count reveals the following stars: 3 diamonds, 8 emeralds, 12 amethysts, 16 sapphires, 24 rubies, 30 topazes. Attempting to touch the stars reveals that they are incorporeal, with the exception of the red stars: these are actually rubies embedded in the walls. 

A detect magic spell will reveal that the arcane glyphs are illusions, as are all the gleaming stars except the rubies. 

Solution 

A character that turns the red dial to the number 24 but leaves the others untouched (following the instructions of the third and eighth word in the inscription: “Count Stars Above Ruby Lights Only”) is rewarded by the opening of a secret nigh undetectable door on the western side of the chamber 

If any of the other dials are adjusted from the zero position, the door remains inactive until they are reset to their nominal position. 

Hint Checks 

If the players are having difficulty solving the puzzle, their characters can make checks to receive hints: 

Wisdom (Insight) DC 15. The inscription seems to provide meaningless instruction, but it does appear to be worded specifically to feature three lines with eight words each. 

Intelligence (Investigation) DC 10. The carved words in the walls appear to emphasize the third and eighth words in each line of the inscription. 

Intelligence (History) DC 15. Many scholars agree that fey creatures place unusual importance on numerology, and there is often hidden significance in them.
