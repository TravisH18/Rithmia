# List of possible one shots
May be expanded on individual pages if necessary

I'll try to give a brief description of theme, genre, then give possible details where you, the GM, can expand on and make it your own. For the most part monsters can be scaled to fit any level of party you would like to choose. These can be fit in to campaigns or run as one shots

## Classic Dungeon Crawlers
These are one-shots where your party is a classic group of adventurers that are hired to dive in to dungeons where the average person would not survive.
- Collapsed Mine:
    - In a mining town the party is hired to explore a mine where the tunnels have caved in.
    - You can have this be urgent and have the party be the muscle in a rescue party.
    - Or this can be a week or so post-collapse and the party can be going in to investigate the cave in and discover what caused it. Maybe some creature is digging its way back up from below. The party goes searching for survivors and finds out what the mine woke up instead.
- Drowned temple:
    - Drought or some other disaster has causes lake level to drop and reveals an ancient temple nobody knew was there. 
    - Party can be hired to check it out or it can be a mad dash for treasure against a rival party.

## Urban dungeon crawls
These are similar to the section above but take place above ground or in crowded city areas.

- Wizard's estate sale
    - A dead wizard's toweer is being "liquidated". The party is hired to clear it room by room.
        - Lot's of fun possiblities hear for players to disarm magic traps, fight animated creatures like animated armor, flying swords, and hommunculus, etc. 
        - Can be a maze in itself like a tower that rearranges itself.
        - Not everything in the tower knows the owner is dead so these combat encounters can turn in to social encounters where they convince the creatures to stop fighting because the owner isn't coming back.
        - Have fun coming up with the mad mage's tower antics and twists.
 
## Mysteries and Investigations
Lower on combat, high on table talk. Great for shorter one - shots or for groups that like the social, role-play parts of the game and aren't big on combat.
- Locked Room Murder:
    - A noble or some other wealthy dignatary is found dead inside a sealed study at / after a dinner party. The party are all guests with varying level of relation to the host / victim. The doors are watched, everyone has a motive. They have until morning before teh guard arrives and arrests the most convenient suspect.
    - Very much Knives out and Clue inspired
- The Lost Day
    - Party arrives in town where everyone thinks it is one day earlier than it actually is. Turns out the entire population has lost / forgotten the same single day. Something happened in that gap, someone was paid good money to erase memories. 
    - The party has to track down who erased memories and / or who did something during this day. 
- The Body Swap
    - A trusted figure in town has been replaced by a double. The party knows because the real one hired them by letter before they vanished. "I know they are an imposter because I'm the real one, and they want me to disappear." Now they have to prove it before the impostor finishes what it came to do.

## Horror
Dim the lights and lean into the creep. Domains of dread can be great inspiration for this. Especially, the Ravenloft: The Horrors within section on Domains of Ravenloft and the random domains section https://5e.tools/book.html#rhw,2,random%20domains,0 or creating domains of dread https://5e.tools/book.html#rhw,4,creating%20a%20darklord,0
- The cabin in the woods. 
    - The party shelters from a storm in an abandoned hunting lodge. The storm does not pass. The woods get closer. The doors they came in through are not where they left them.
- The harvest festival. 
    - A remote farming village welcomes the party warmly, feeds them well, and is very excited about the ceremony at midnight. Far too excited.
- The thing in the well. 
    - Children have gone missing. The well at the center of town has been boarded over for a generation. The town council would really prefer the party look literally anywhere else.

## Heists
Classic fun. Plan, infiltrate, improvise when it goes wrong because it always goes wrong. Feel free to wait until a plan is hatched to throw your wrench in the pie. The party doesn't always know everything going in.
- The auction. 
    - A cursed item the party needs is going up for auction in a heavily warded manor full of rival bidders. They can buy it, steal it, or convince three other factions to fight over it while they walk out the back.
- The prison break. 
    - Someone the party needs is locked in a fortress that has never lost a prisoner. They have one night, a forged transfer order, and a plan that survives contact with exactly zero guards.
- The moving train. 
    - The score is on a caravan — or an airship, or a literal magic-powered train — that does not stop. The party boards, works their way to the prize, and has to get off before the next checkpoint.
    - Maybe the party needs to move from the front to the back and detach the caboose before the next stop.
    - A specific crate needs to be taken from the airship cargo chamber (party paraglides in or jumps off one ship on to the target vessel)
    - Heroic saving a magic powered run-away train that is being heisted by someone else

## Social Intrigue
These, in my opinion, are better for inserting into an existing campaign where your players care about the NPCs and other parties involved but if your group loves to RP these can work well as individual one shot adventures.
- The peace summit. 
    - Two warring factions meet to sign a treaty, and the party is hired as neutral security. Someone at the table wants the talks to fail, and they have until the signing to find out who.
- The masquerade. 
    - The party must deliver a message — or a knife — to a specific guest at a masked ball without knowing what their target looks like, working only from a riddle and a description of how they dance.
- The inheritance. 
    - A patron has died and named the party in the will, alongside a dozen relatives who are furious about it. To collect, they have to survive a weekend at the family estate while everyone's true intentions surface. The party helped the patron many times in the past and has had varying level of contact with the relatives so they don't have to all be opposing forces. It is there job to make sure they are not cut out of the will through nefarious means in order to pay off debts.

## Survival
- The long night. 
    - The sun did not rise. The party has to reach a far-off beacon and relight it before whatever lives in permanent dark reaches the town first. Pure ticking-clock survival.

## The Weird
Some one shots are fun, weird, and don't fit into other categories.
- The shrinking (or Giant's) dungeon. 
    - The party is magically shrunk and dropped into what is, to them, an enormous dungeon — a kitchen, a garden, a bookshelf. Ordinary objects are deadly terrain. Get big again before the cat notices.
- The time loop. 
    - The party relives the same disastrous hour until they get it right. Every loop they remember a little more, and the table pieces together what they have to do before the loop becomes permanent.
